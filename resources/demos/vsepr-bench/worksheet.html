1Before you open anything

Answer from what you already know and commit it to paper. The point is to have something concrete to compare against later, not to be right now.

Prediction

Water, H2O, and sulfur dioxide, SO2, are both described as bent. Predict the bond angle in each one. Give a number for each, even if you are guessing.

H2O: __________°      SO2: __________°

Write one or two sentences saying how you arrived at those numbers.

4Reading it the way a question would

Exam questions run this backwards: they hand you a shape or a formula and expect the count. Work each one out on paper first, then set the sliders and check.

a. A molecule is bent with a bond angle near 118°. How many electron groups surround the central atom, and how many are lone pairs? Set the sliders to your answer and copy the Example readout — both the formula and the line beneath it.

b. A molecule is described as AX3E. Give its electron-pair geometry, its molecular geometry and its approximate bond angle without touching the demonstration, then check by setting the sliders. The AX notation is printed under the Lone pairs count.

c. Press CH4 and read the caption underneath the molecule. It warns you about measuring the drawing with a protractor. What angle does the flat drawing read, what is the true angle, and why do the two differ?

d. Press SO2. The Actual readout and the Example readout do not quote the same number. Write both. Using the Honest limits panel, give one reason a measured angle can sit away from the value this model predicts.

5Solve these without the demonstration

Close the page or look away. Draw the Lewis structure, count the electron groups, then answer. Show your working. Reopen the demonstration afterwards and check yourself — if a prediction missed, write down which step it went wrong at.

1. CCl4. Give the number of electron groups on the central carbon, the electron-pair geometry, the molecular geometry and the bond angle.

2. HCN, with carbon as the central atom. Give the number of electron groups, both geometries and the bond angle. Be explicit about how you counted the triple bond.

3. The nitrite ion, NO2−. Draw the Lewis structure, then give the electron-pair geometry, the molecular geometry and the approximate bond angle.

4. The H–C–H angle in CH4 is 109.5°; the H–N–H angle in NH3 is about 107°. Both central atoms carry four electron groups. Explain the difference, and state the electron-pair geometry of each. No calculation — this one is answered in sentences.

6On your own

Nothing here is graded, and the demonstration does more than this worksheet used.

  • Press Generic labels (A, X) and work through every setting the two sliders allow — there are nine. Write the AX formula for each. Three of them refuse to quote a bond angle; find all three and say what they have in common.
  • Untick Show the electron-geometry frame and tick it again at 3 groups and then at 4 groups. Watch what the frame is drawn between, and work out from the legend why some bonds are wedges and some are dashed.
  • Read the Honest limits panel. H2O is 104.5° but H2S is 92.1°; NH3 is 107° but PH3 is 93.5°. What information about the atoms does VSEPR never use, and which part of a prediction does it still get right?
  • Tap each of the six shapes in the gallery and read the row headings above them. Which electron-pair geometry produces the most molecular geometries, and why that one?

3What each inhibitor actually moves

Now vary one thing at a time: the dose of one inhibitor, with [S] held fixed. Keep [S] at 2.00 mM for both tables — press Reset all between them if the slider drifts.

  1. Tick Competitive inhibitor. Its slider unlocks; it runs 0 to 10.0 mM in steps of 0.1, and Ki = 2.0 mM.
  2. Set [I] competitive to each dose in the table and record the two headline tiles and Rate v now. For the 0 mM row, simply leave the checkbox unticked.
  3. Untick it, tick Noncompetitive inhibitor instead, and repeat.

a. In the competitive table, Km at [I] = 10.0 mM is how many times Km at [I] = 0? Show that Km,app = Km(1 + [I]/Ki) gives the same factor.

b. State the rule in your own words: one sentence for what a competitive inhibitor does to Vmax and Km, one sentence for the noncompetitive one.

c. Press the Competitive preset button — it sets that inhibitor to 10.0 mM and clears the other — then press Flood with substrate and read the tile labelled Rate at saturating [S] (500 mM), as a share of the uninhibited Vmax. Now press Noncompetitive and read the same tile.

Competitive: __________ %     Noncompetitive: __________ %

Compare both numbers with your prediction in Part 1. If either was off, say specifically which assumption produced it.

The competitive figure is not quite 100%, and it never will be at any finite [S]. The curve approaches the uninhibited Vmax as an asymptote — 500 mM is close enough to make the point, not close enough to reach it.

4Using it the way a question would

  2. Condition A: tick Competitive inhibitor and set it to 6.0 mM. Record the row.

5Solve these without the demonstration

Close the page or look away. Show your working. Then reopen it, reproduce each situation on the sliders, and write down where any disagreement came from. Unless a problem says otherwise, use Vmax = 100 µmol L−1 min−1, Km = 2.00 mM, and Ki = 2.0 mM for both inhibitors.

1. Calculate the initial rate at [S] = 3.00 mM with no inhibitor present, and express it as a percentage of Vmax.

2. A competitive inhibitor is added at [I] = 5.0 mM. Predict Km,app, then predict the initial rate at [S] = 4.00 mM.

3. A double-reciprocal plot of two conditions gives two straight lines. Both cross the vertical axis at 1/v = 0.0100 L min µmol−1. One crosses the horizontal axis at −0.500 mM−1, the other at −0.125 mM−1. Identify the inhibitor type, find Vmax and both Km values, and calculate [I].

4. A noncompetitive inhibitor is present at a concentration that halves the rate at every [S]. State that concentration. Then explain, in terms of what the inhibitor does to individual enzyme molecules, why a solution containing half as much enzyme and no inhibitor at all would give an identical v-versus-[S] curve.

6On your own

Nothing here is graded, and the demonstration does considerably more than this worksheet used.

  • Drag Temperature across its whole range and watch the left-hand environment plot. Find the two temperatures at which activity is half-maximal. The peak is not halfway between them — work out which of the two effects, the Arrhenius rise or the loss of folded enzyme, makes the curve asymmetric.
  • Take the temperature to 55 °C, then bring it back to 37 °C. The rate returns. Read the Honest limits panel and say what would have to be added to the model for that recovery to stop happening.
  • Press Pepsin, opt 2.0, then drag pH back to 7.0 and watch the Activity vs 37 °C, pH 7 tile. Do the same starting from Trypsin, opt 8.0. Explain what this predicts about where in the body each enzyme can work.
  • Switch on both inhibitors at once and open the 1/v vs 1/[S] tab. Both intercepts move. Predict where each intercept lands before you look.

Where the model stops

Every ratio here assumes the two genes sit on different chromosomes (independent assortment), that all 16 gamete pairings are equally likely, and that you have counted many offspring. A real litter of 8 puppies almost never lands on 9 : 3 : 4. The block colors mark phenotype groups; they are not the organism's actual colors.

What this is for

The reading this is meant to correct: a cross that comes out 9:7 or 12:3:1 or 9:3:4 looks like a Punnett square behaving differently — as if epistasis and other non-Mendelian traits ran on some replacement rule. That is a fair thing to conclude from the numbers alone, since the numbers really are different. But nothing about the square changed. Every cross on this page is the same AaBb × AaBb, the same four gamete types, the same sixteen boxes.

What to notice: click through all six dihybrid presets and keep one eye on the blue Genotype tally. It reads 9 : 3 : 3 : 1 every single time, and the nine individual genotypes stay at 1:2:1:2:4:2:1:2:1. Only the colors move. A phenotype ratio is not produced by the square — it is produced by biochemistry deciding which genotypes end up looking alike. Notice too that "complementary genes" and "duplicate recessive" give the identical 9:7. The square cannot tell those two mechanisms apart; only an experiment on the actual pathway can.

The sex-linked tab is the exception that proves the rule. There the genotype tally does change when you swap the parents — not because the square changed its behavior, but because the gametes going into it changed. A father hands his single X to every daughter and his Y to every son. So a white-eyed mother crossed with a red-eyed father gives white eyes to every son and red eyes to every daughter, while the reverse cross — white-eyed father, red-eyed mother with two normal alleles — gives a whole generation with red eyes. (Give that mother one hidden white allele instead and half her sons go white again, because now she has one to hand down. Try it.) Remember that males are hemizygous: XaY is not "homozygous recessive," it is one allele and one Y that carries no copy of this gene at all.

1Before you open anything

Answer from what you already know. Being right is not the point — having something on paper to argue with later is.

Prediction

In this model, one locus (Aa × Aa) produces three possible offspring heights spread across 150–190 cm. Now suppose eight unlinked loci share the job, each capital allele adding the same amount, and the trait still spans 150–190 cm. With no environmental effect at all, how many distinct heights can the offspring have?

Write one sentence saying how you got that number.

4Reading it backwards, and one shortcut that fails

Exam questions rarely hand you n. They hand you something the distribution does, and expect n back.

  1. A cross of this kind produces 11 evenly spaced phenotype classes, 4.00 cm apart, across 150–190 cm. Work out n on paper first, then set the slider and check.

a. n = ____________   Which of the two clues did you use, and would the other one alone have been enough?

Now a claim worth testing: more loci contributing to a trait means more of its variation is genetic, so heritability goes up. It sounds reasonable. Test it.

b. Which way did h2 move? Since σE never changed, the only term that moved was VG. Say what happened to it and why, and then state what the claim above got wrong.

c. That result depends on a modelling choice, not on biology: adding loci here divides a fixed 150–190 cm range into finer steps rather than widening the range. Read the What the model assumes panel and name one other simplification that this demonstration makes.

d. With n = 2, find the setting of σE that makes h2 read exactly 0.50. Then, before touching the slider, predict the value that would do the same at n = 8, and check it.

σE at n = 2: ____________ cm    predicted at n = 8: ____________ cm    actual: ____________ cm

Watch the wording. Every h2 on this page describes the variation among this set of offspring. It never says that some fraction of one individual's height came from their genes. That second question has no answer, because no part of anyone's height was built without both.

5Solve these without the demonstration

Close the page or look away. Show your working. Then reopen it, check yourself, and if a number is off, write down which step produced it.

1. Three loci contribute additively to a trait spanning 150–190 cm, all parents fully heterozygous, no environmental effect. Give the number of phenotype classes, the gap between neighbouring classes, and the fraction of offspring with the all-lowercase genotype.

2. Five loci, same trait, with an environmental SD of 6.00 cm. Find VG, VE, the total SD, and h2 to two decimal places. (Genetic variance in this model is 200/n cm².)

3. Population A: one locus, no environmental variance. Population B: eight loci, σE = 12.00 cm. Which population's offspring are more spread out in height? Compute both total SDs before answering, then say why comparing total SD across two populations tells you nothing on its own about which one experiences more environmental variance.

4. No calculation for this one. A study of a plant population reports h2 = 0.53 for stem height. Explain what that number does say, and state one conclusion people commonly draw from a heritability estimate that it does not support. Then name one condition that has to hold for the estimate to describe any population other than the one measured.

6On your own

Nothing here is graded, and the worksheet has deliberately left these alone.

  • The Overlay normal approximation checkbox stays locked below n = 4. Turn it on at n = 4 and again at n = 8 and find where the dashed curve sits above or below the bars. The bars are the exact binomial; the curve is only an approximation to it.
  • The dots along the bottom are 260 sampled offspring, and New sample draws a fresh set. At n = 8 with σE = 0, how many samples do you get through before one offspring lands in an extreme class? Compare that with the frequency the panel reports.
  • Set n = 2 and raise σE one step at a time from 0.00. Find the setting at which the caption stops describing the classes as separate humps, and check it against half the gap between classes. Then explain why half the gap is the sensible place for that boundary.

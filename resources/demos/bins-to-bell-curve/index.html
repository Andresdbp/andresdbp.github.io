What the model assumes

Simplified on purpose. Every locus has the same effect size, the alleles are purely additive (no dominance, no epistasis), the loci are unlinked, and the environmental deviate is independent of genotype. Real height is messier — but the shape argument survives all of that.

What this is for

The misconception: students can recite "height is polygenic" but, asked why more genes give a smooth bell instead of just more boxes, they say more genes means more categories — as if 8 loci produced 8 chunky steps. And they treat environment as random noise sitting on top of the "real" genetic answer.

What to notice: three things happen at once as you add loci, and it takes all three. The number of classes grows (2n+1: 3, 5, 7, … 17), the gap between neighbouring classes shrinks (20/n cm, so 20 cm becomes 2.5 cm), and the extreme classes collapse in frequency — (1/4)n takes 1 in 4 offspring down to 1 in 65,536. Many classes, packed close, rare at the edges and common in the middle: that is a bell. Count the bars at n = 1 and 2 and check them against 3 and 5 yourself.

The binomial is not the normal curve. This distribution is exactly binomial — it only approaches a bell as n grows, which is why the smooth overlay stays locked until n = 4. At one locus there is no bell to draw, just three spikes.

Environment is variance, not noise. Push σE up with n = 2 and the five separate spikes merge into one smooth hump: two offspring with different genotypes now land at the same height, and you could not sort them by eye. Heritability drops because VE grew, not because the genes changed. And read h2 carefully — it says what fraction of the variation in this population is genetic. It never says that h2 of any one person's height is "from their genes"; that question does not have an answer.

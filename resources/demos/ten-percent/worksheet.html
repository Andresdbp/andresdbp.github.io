1Before you open anything

Answer from what you already know. Being wrong here costs nothing — the point is to have a number on paper that the demonstration can settle.

Prediction

A food chain has 5 trophic levels. The producers fix 10,000 kJ m−2 yr−1. Transfer efficiency is raised from 10% to 20% — doubled. By what factor does the energy reaching the top level change?

Write one sentence saying how you got that factor.

d. The shortcut "the bigger bar holds more energy" gives the wrong answer here. Using the turnover times, explain in two sentences how the zooplankton can carry five times the standing mass while receiving less than a quarter of the annual production.

The standing-crop figures are the classic English Channel values and the turnover times are rounded approximations that shift with season and species. Treat the production row as an order-of-magnitude estimate rather than a measurement, and do not quote it to three figures in an answer.

5Solve these without the demonstration

Close the page or look away. Show your working. Then reopen it, check yourself, and if a number is off, write down exactly where.

1. Producers fix 20,000 kJ m−2 yr−1. The chain has 4 trophic levels and transfer efficiency is 10% at every step. How much energy reaches the top level, and what share of the producers' energy is that?

2. A 5-level chain starts from 10,000 kJ m−2 yr−1 of production and delivers 16 kJ m−2 yr−1 to the top level. Find the transfer efficiency, assuming it is the same at every step.

3. A 100 km2 reserve (1×108 m2) has 5 trophic levels, 10% efficiency and 10,000 kJ m−2 yr−1 of net primary production. A 20 kg carnivore needs about 2.53×106 kJ per year. How many can the reserve support? Watch your units.

4. No arithmetic for this one. A survey of a lake reports more zooplankton biomass than phytoplankton biomass, and a separate survey of the same lake reports annual phytoplankton production far above annual zooplankton production. Explain why both reports can be correct at once, and state which of the two quantities an energy pyramid is built from and why that one can never invert.

6On your own

Nothing here is graded, and the demonstration does more than this worksheet uses.

  • Watch the Why the exponential is the whole lesson panel while you switch between 3, 4 and 5 levels. The multiplier printed there is 4 raised to the number of transfers. Work out why the base is 4 and not 15, then argue from that number why real food chains rarely run past four or five links.
  • The note under Predators reports a density per km2 and compares it with the 0.01–0.5 per km2 that real 20 kg carnivores live at. Find combinations of efficiency, NPP and chain length that land inside that realistic range, and see how narrow the window is.
  • The hint under Producer energy (NPP) names a biome and converts the value to grams of dry plant matter per m2 per year at 18 kJ g−1. Set it to a desert value and a rainforest value and decide how much of the difference between those two ecosystems' top predators is production and how much is efficiency.

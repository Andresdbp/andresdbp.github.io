1Before you open anything

Answer from what you already know, then leave it alone. The point is to have a number on paper that the demonstration can later agree or disagree with.

Prediction

Glucose enters a cell through carrier proteins in the membrane. The cell starts empty of glucose, and the fluid outside holds 50 mM glucose. You now double the outside concentration to 100 mM. By what factor does the rate of glucose entry change?

Write one sentence saying how you got that factor.

2Reading the bench when it has settled

Open the demonstration. Three lanes run side by side across one membrane, and each has its own control panel on the right.

  1. Press Reset bench. Every slider returns to its starting value and the model runs.
  2. Leave it alone for about two minutes, until the readouts stop changing. Do not touch a slider while you wait.
  3. Fill in the table from the three route panels. Copy the numbers exactly as displayed, including the words underneath the Net flux tiles.

a. Two lanes finished with a net flux of zero and one did not finish at all. Which lane is still moving solute, and what is it spending to do it?

b. In the O2 panel, Net flux reads zero while Crossing in → and Crossing out ← are both clearly non-zero and equal. Write one sentence saying what quantity the word equilibrium is a statement about.

c. In the Na+ panel, Pump rate, out ↑ and Leak, net in ↓ read the same number, so [Na+] inside is also holding still. Explain how this steady state differs from the one in the O2 lane, using the ATP charge reading in your answer.

The Honest limits panel tells you what the model is holding fixed. The outside is treated as a large bath, so the outside sliders never drift — only the cell interior moves. The cell is one well-mixed compartment of 1 pL, which is why a flux of 1 fmol s−1 changes the inside by exactly 1 mM s−1. All three solutes share one 0–200 mM scale so the routes can be compared; real extracellular values are about 145 mM Na+, 5 mM glucose and 0.1 mM O2.

3What happens when you keep raising the concentration

Now hold the model still and change one thing at a time. Both passive lanes will be set to an empty cell, which is the same situation the flux chart draws.

  1. Press Pause in the Bench panel. The button now reads Run, and nothing will drift while you work.
  2. Drag [O2] inside to 0.0 mM and [glucose] inside to 0.0 mM.
  3. Check that Carrier proteins reads 100. Leave it there for the whole table.
  4. Set [O2] outside and [glucose] outside both to 20 mM, and record the two Net flux values and Carriers occupied.

4.1 — Working backwards to a carrier number. Stay paused. Set [glucose] inside to 0.0 mM and [glucose] outside to 45 mM.

5Solve these without the demonstration

Close the page or look away from it. Show your working. Then reopen it, set the stated values, and check — and where you were off, write down which step went wrong.

1. A membrane has P = 0.10 fmol s−1 mM−1 for O2, with 160 mM outside and 40 mM inside. Find the unidirectional flux inward, the unidirectional flux outward, and the net flux. Then raise both concentrations by 30 mM and state what happens to each of the three numbers.

2. A membrane carries 75 carrier proteins, each turning over 0.08 fmol s−1 at full occupancy, with Km = 15 mM. Glucose is 60 mM outside and 10 mM inside. Find Vmax, the net flux, and the fraction of carriers occupied.

3. An empty cell ([inside] = 0) has both routes available: simple diffusion with P = 0.10, and 100 carriers with Km = 15 mM. Find the [outside] at which the two routes carry exactly the same flux. Then predict, with a calculation, where that crossing moves if the carrier count is doubled to 200.

4. A cell is treated with a poison that blocks ATP synthesis completely. Predict what happens over the next few minutes to (i) the rate of O2 entry, (ii) the rate of glucose entry through its carrier, and (iii) [Na+] inside. Explain why the presence of a protein in the glucose route does not change your answer, and name what the ATP was buying in the one route that does change.

6On your own

Nothing here is graded. The demonstration does considerably more than this worksheet uses.

  • Switch the chart to Concentrations over time, press Cut ATP supply, and time how long the Na+ trace takes to close half the gap to its dashed outside line. Compare with the figure quoted in the caption.
  • Uncheck Bilayer is permeable to this molecule in the O2 panel. That models a charged ion facing bare lipid. Then uncheck Carrier protein present in the membrane mid-run and watch where the glucose lane freezes.
  • Set ATP supply to 2.0, then 5.0, then 20.0 % s−1, letting each settle. Record the gradient each time. Is the gradient proportional to the supply?
  • Press Equalise all sides and watch all three lanes. Only one rebuilds a gradient. Then read the warning at the bottom of Honest limits: what real limit on the pump is this model leaving out?

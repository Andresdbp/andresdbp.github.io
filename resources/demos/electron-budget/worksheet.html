4When “give everyone an octet” runs out

The usual shortcut is to hand every atom eight electrons and hope the total works out. Here is a molecule where it does not, and the repair that a test question expects.

  1. Choose CO₂ — Carbon dioxide. Record the budget.
  2. Click each dashed line once, giving two single bonds.
  3. Click each oxygen three times, giving each one three lone pairs. Fill in the first row below.
  4. Repair it: right-click (or press and hold) one oxygen once to take a lone pair back, then click the bond to that oxygen to make it a double bond. Do the same on the other side. Fill in the second row.
  5. Press Check.

5Solve these without the demonstration

Close the page, or look away from it. Show your working. Then reopen it, check yourself, and if a number is off, write down where the count went wrong.

1. Find the electron budget for the chlorate ion, ClO3−. State how many pairs that is.

2. The phosphate ion, PO43−, is drawn with phosphorus in the centre, four single P–O bonds and three lone pairs on every oxygen. Find the budget, show that this drawing spends it exactly, and give the formal charge on phosphorus and on each oxygen. Check that they add to the ion's charge.

3. Hydrogen cyanide is HCN, with carbon in the middle. Find the budget, then give a structure in which carbon and nitrogen both reach eight and hydrogen reaches two. State how many of the electrons end up in bonds and how many end up as lone pairs.

4. In the demonstration, phosphorus in PCl5 is labelled expanded, 10 and is accepted. A drawing that put 10 electrons on nitrogen would be labelled impossible. Explain the difference between the two atoms, and state the counting rule that never bends.

6On your own

Nothing here is graded. The demonstration holds more than this worksheet uses.

  • Load NO — Nitric oxide. Its budget is odd, and Place single electrons switches itself on. Work out for yourself why no arrangement of pairs can ever total 11, and why a triple bond does not rescue it.
  • Build the accepted structure for O₂ — Oxygen and read the message. It satisfies every rule in this unit and still disagrees with a laboratory magnet. Decide what that costs the model.
  • Load NO₃⁻ — Nitrate ion and build it three times, putting the double bond on a different oxygen each time. Turn on Show formal charges on the drawing and compare. Which drawing is the real ion?
  • Type a formula containing a transition metal, such as FeCl3. Read what the demonstration refuses to do, and work out why counting valence electrons this way stops working outside the main group.

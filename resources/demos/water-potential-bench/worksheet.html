1Before you open anything

Answer from what you already know. Commit to it in ink — the point is to have something on paper that the demonstration can later confirm or contradict.

Prediction

A U-tube is divided at the bottom by a membrane that water crosses and solute never does. The left arm is filled with 5.0 mM sucrose, the right arm with pure water. Both arms start with liquid columns 150 cm tall. The tube is left alone until net water movement stops.

At that moment, what is the sucrose concentration in each arm?

Write one sentence saying what reasoning gave you those two numbers.

5Solve these without the demonstration

Close the page, or look away from it. Show your working, and carry units. Then reopen it, check yourself, and if a number is off, write down which step produced the error. Use R = 0.0831 L·bar·mol−1·K−1 and ρg = 0.000981 bar per cm of water column.

1. Find Ψs for a 0.150 M NaCl solution in an open beaker at 300 K, then state Ψ for that solution. To check: Cells tab, NaCl, Temperature T = 300 K, Bath concentration = 0.150 M, read the Bath column.

2. A plant cell is flaccid: Ψp = 0 and Ψs = −9.81 bar. It is placed in an open beaker of 0.400 M sucrose at 295 K. Compute Ψ for the cell and Ψ for the solution, then predict the direction of net water movement and the cell's final volume relative to its present one. To check: Cells tab, sucrose, 295 K, preset Plant just goes flaccid.

3. In the U-tube at 295 K, the left arm holds sucrose and the right arm holds pure water. The two arms together always contain 300 cm of column. At rest the left column stands at 218.6 cm.

(a) Find Ψp for each arm. (b) Using the fact that Ψ must match across the membrane, find Ψs in the left arm. (c) From that, find the left arm's sucrose concentration in mM. (d) State the right arm's concentration and comment on the pair. To check: left arm 8.0 mM, right arm 0.0 mM, Refill level, Jump to equilibrium.

4. An animal cell and a plant cell are both dropped into distilled water. Using Ψ = Ψp + Ψs, explain why the plant cell reaches an equilibrium volume and the animal cell has none available to it. Your answer must say what value Ψp is confined to in the animal cell and why that confinement matters.

6On your own

Nothing here is graded, and none of it appeared above. The demonstration is worth more than this worksheet asked of it.

  • Hold the U-tube at 5.0 mM against pure water and drag Temperature T from 278 K to 313 K, re-equilibrating at each end. Which way does the equilibrium height difference move, and does the RT value in the hint account for the whole change?
  • Give both arms solute — say 8.0 mM against 3.0 mM. Does the tube settle at the same place as 5.0 mM against 0.0 mM? Decide from the run whether the difference or the ratio of the two concentrations is what governs the endpoint.
  • On the Cells tab with sucrose, find the highest Bath concentration at which the animal cell still bursts. Then work out from the lysis threshold quoted in the panel why that particular value is the boundary.
  • Read the panel headed Where this model stops being true. Pick the one simplification you think would most change the numbers you recorded today, and say why.

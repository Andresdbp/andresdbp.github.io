1Before you open anything

Answer from what you already know. Being right is not the point — the point is to have a number on paper that the projection can later confirm or contradict.

Prediction

A country of 100.0 million people has a wide-based age pyramid: 34% of everyone alive is under 15. Today its fertility drops from 3.91 children per woman to replacement, 2.1. Nobody enters or leaves the country. What is its population 70 years from today? Give a number in millions.

Write one or two sentences on the reasoning that produced that number.

a. The New fertility (TFR) slider never moved off 2.10, and this model has no migration at all. So where did the extra people between year 0 and year 60 come from?

b. Fertility is fixed at 2.10 children per woman for the whole run, yet the Women aged 15–49 column changes a great deal. Births in any five-year step are the fertility rate multiplied by the size of that pool. Use that to explain why total births keep rising after year 0.

The population is scaled to start at exactly 100 million so percentages read straight off the numbers. The units are arbitrary; only the ratios carry meaning. Nothing in this model depends on the country being that size.

4Working backwards, and a shortcut that fails

4A — hit a target. You want a scenario that stores at least +100% of growth: a population that doubles after fertility has already reached replacement.

  1. Press Reset everything, then drag Shape all the way right. Record Growth still to come from panel 3, together with the two population figures printed directly beneath that percentage. Can the shape slider alone reach +100%?
  2. Press Expansive to come back to 3.91. Now raise Phase-in time one step at a time until Growth still to come first passes +100%.

a. A phase-in of several decades is a slower fall in fertility, so it might read as a smaller effect. It stores more growth instead. Explain why, in terms of the birth cohorts arriving while fertility is still on its way down.

4B — the shortcut. A common and perfectly reasonable rule of thumb: fertility below 2.1 means the population shrinks. Test it on this pyramid.

5Solve these without the demonstration

Close the page or look away from it. Show your working. Then reopen it, check yourself, and if a number is off, write down where.

1. A country of 400 million people has an age pyramid the same shape as the Expansive preset, and its fertility falls to replacement this year. Using the stored-growth percentage you recorded in part 3, predict its population when growth stops, and state roughly how many years that takes. Explain in one line why the answer scales this way when the model was built at 100 million.

2. On the Expansive run, the pool of women aged 15–49 goes from 22.6 million at year 0 to 33.7 million at year 40, with fertility held at 2.10 throughout. By what factor does the annual number of births change over that period? Then find the fertility that would have been needed at year 40 to hold births at their year-0 level.

3. Replacement fertility in this model is 2.09, not 2.00. Give the two reasons, and state which way the replacement value moves in a country with higher child mortality. No calculation — this one is an explanation.

4. A country whose pyramid matches the Contracting preset raises its fertility to replacement, 2.10. Predict whether its population levels off immediately, and give the direction and rough size of the change that follows. Justify it by saying which age groups hold this pyramid's largest cohorts.

6On your own

Nothing here is graded, and the worksheet has used only part of what the demonstration does.

  • The Change happens at year slider has not been touched yet. Reset, then move it to 25 and to 50. Watch which readouts in panel 3 change and which do not, and work out why the percentage behaves differently from the two population figures under it.
  • Switch the pyramid between Share of population (%) and People (millions) and press Play in each. The bottom rows do something quite different in the two views. Decide what each view hides.
  • Set New fertility (TFR) to 3.00 and read what panel 3 reports instead of a number. Then compare the solid curve with the dashed if fertility had never changed line at year 60, and say what the gap between them is measuring.

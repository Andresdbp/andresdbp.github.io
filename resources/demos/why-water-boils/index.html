Read this before you conclude anything

The dashed line is an extrapolation, not a measurement. Nobody has ever measured water boiling at −73 °C, because water always has hydrogen bonds. Different reasonable fits (molar mass vs. period number, three anchor points vs. two) put the estimate anywhere from about −70 to −100 °C. Treat it as an order-of-magnitude argument: the anomaly is hundreds of degrees, not tens.

What this is for

The misconception: students learn "bigger molecules boil at higher temperatures" and then cannot say why water — the lightest molecule in its column — boils about 173 °C above where its own family's trend line lands. Some students go the other way after seeing the anomaly and decide molar mass does not matter at all. Both conclusions are wrong.

What to notice, part one: turn on group 14 by itself. CH4, SiH4, GeH4, SnH4 climb steadily with molar mass. Nothing in that series can hydrogen bond, so the only thing holding the molecules together is London dispersion — and dispersion forces get stronger as a molecule gets bigger and has more electrons to slosh around. The mass rule is real. Now check groups 15, 16 and 17: from the second member onward every one of them does the same thing. Heavier really does mean higher.

What to notice, part two: now look at the first member of each of those three groups. NH3, H2O and HF sit far above the line their own heavier relatives define (+71, +173 and +116 °C). Those are the three hydrides where H is bonded to N, O or F — small, very electronegative atoms with lone pairs. Hydrogen bonding is an extra attraction stacked on top of dispersion, not a replacement for it. That is why the fix is "water boils 173 °C higher than the trend predicts", and not "the trend is fake".

The honest caveat: CH4 also misses its own extrapolated line — by −37 °C, in the downward direction. Boiling point versus molar mass is not exactly a straight line even with only one kind of force acting, so a fit through three points will never land perfectly on a fourth. That miss is the size of the ordinary scatter in this kind of plot. Water's miss is almost five times larger and points the other way. That contrast — sign and size — is the evidence, not the precise value of −73 °C.

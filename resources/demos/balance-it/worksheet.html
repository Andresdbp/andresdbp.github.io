The formulas themselves have no controls. There is no way to turn H2O into H2O2 on this page, because there is no such move in chemistry either: the formula of a compound is settled before the equation is written. H2O2 is a different substance, and it has its own reaction in the picker.

5Solve these without the demonstration

Close the page, or look away from it. Show your working — carbon and metals first, hydrogen next, oxygen last. Then reopen the demonstration, build each one, and if you were wrong write down exactly where.

1. Balance, in lowest whole-number terms: C3H8 + O2 → CO2 + H2O

2. Balance, in lowest whole-number terms: C2H6 + O2 → CO2 + H2O. Balancing carbon and hydrogen first leaves an oxygen count that cannot be reached with a whole coefficient on O2. Say what you did about that, and why it is allowed.

3. Someone submits 4 KClO3 → 4 KCl + 6 O2. Check each element and say whether the atoms are conserved. Then say whether the answer would be accepted, and write the form a chemist would write. Explain the difference between the two questions you just answered.

4. For 3 Ca(NO3)2 + 2 Na3PO4, find the total number of oxygen atoms on that side. Show the two separate contributions, and state why the answer is not 3 × 3 = 9.

6On your own

Nothing here is graded. The demonstration holds twelve reactions and this worksheet used four of them.

  • Take Aluminum corrodes in air and try to balance it with an odd coefficient on Al2O3. Work out from the atom table why no odd value can ever work, then find the answer.
  • Watch the Particle view while you press +. Nothing about any drawn unit changes shape; another identical copy appears. Write one sentence connecting that to why a coefficient can never break a substance apart.
  • Work the two single-replacement reactions, Zinc in hydrochloric acid and Aluminum in copper(II) chloride, and describe what the second one demands that the first one does not.
  • Drive Balanced so far to 12 / 12 without using Show the answer once.

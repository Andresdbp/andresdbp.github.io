1Before you open anything

Answer from what you already know. The point is to have something on paper to argue with later, not to be right now.

Prediction

A population of finches has a mean beak depth of 10.000 mm. Deeper beaks crack the available seeds better, so the birds that survive to breed are not a random sample: they average 10.200 mm. Predict the mean beak depth of their offspring generation.

Write one or two sentences saying how you got that number, and name anything you had to assume to get there.

2Setting up a directional run

Open the demonstration. Three mode tabs sit above the graph; the sliders and the readout tiles are down the right-hand side (underneath the graph on a phone).

  1. Set Heritability h² to 0.50 first. This slider restarts the population at generation 0 every time you move it, so it goes first or it will undo your other settings.
  2. Set Strength of selection to 0.40.
  3. Click the Directional tab.
  4. Press Reset. The Generation tile should read 0.
  5. Record the row for generation 0, then press Step generation once and record generation 1. Keep stepping to fill the rest.

3What decides how much of S survives

Now change one thing only. Leave the mode on Directional and the strength on 0.40, and vary heritability.

  1. Set Heritability h² to 0.00. The population restarts at generation 0 on its own.
  2. With Generation reading 0, record S, R, and the Predicted h² × S tile.

a. One column is identical in all five rows. Which one, and what does that tell you about whether heritability affects who breeds?

b. Write the rule connecting R, S and h² as an equation, then write it again as one sentence in your own words.

c. Go back to your prediction in Part 1. The breeders averaged 10.200 mm against a population mean of 10.000 mm — that is exactly the S you recorded. Which row of this table matches the answer you predicted? What did you assume h² was?

With h² = 0, selection is still working: the orange breeders curve is visibly to the right of the blue histogram and S is not zero. What is missing is heritable variation, so none of the advantage the breeders had is transmitted. Selection without heritable variation is not evolution.

4Working backwards, and one place the shortcut fails

Backwards. A question is more likely to hand you the response and ask for the heritability than the other way round.

  1. Set Strength of selection to 0.60, mode Directional, and press Reset. Record S at generation 0: ____________ mm.
  2. On paper, work out the heritability that would give a response of exactly R = 0.150 mm with that S. Show the working here.
  1. Set Heritability h² to the value you calculated and read the R tile. Did it come out at 0.150 mm? If not, find your error before going on.

Where a reasonable shortcut breaks. “R = 0, so nothing is happening” is a natural thing to conclude. Test it.

  1. Set Heritability h² to 0.50 and Strength of selection to 0.40.
  2. Click the Stabilizing tab and press Reset.
  3. Record generation 0, then press Step generation until you have every row. The h² right now tile is the fourth one in The two numbers to watch.

a. S is zero at every generation, and the fitness curve is nowhere near flat. Explain why S comes out zero here, in terms of where the green fitness curve peaks relative to the population.

b. R is also zero, and the mean never leaves 10.000 mm. State one quantity in your table that did change, by how much between generation 0 and generation 10, and argue whether the population evolved.

c. The h² right now tile falls over the run even though you never touched the slider. Its subtitle shows the two numbers it is built from. Which of those two is dropping, and why does that make sense for selection that removes the extremes?

The population in this model is infinite, so nothing you see is sampling luck — every change is selection. There is also no mutation, so variance that stabilizing selection removes is gone for good and the run bottoms out at the non-heritable floor. In a real population mutation keeps feeding variance back in, and the two forces settle at a balance well above that floor.

5Solve these without the demonstration

Close the page or look away from it. Show your working. Then reopen it, set the controls each question describes, and check yourself — if you were wrong, write down where it went off.

1. Directional selection, strength 0.40, heritability 0.75. At generation 0 the population mean is 10.000 mm and S = 0.200 mm. Find R, then predict the mean beak depth at generation 4. State the assumption about S you had to make to get from R to generation 4.

2. In a different run the population mean is 10.000 mm, the birds that breed average 10.300 mm, and the following generation averages 10.180 mm. Find S, find R, and find h². Then say which mode and which two slider settings would reproduce this in the demonstration.

3. Over ten generations of stabilizing selection the mean beak depth is 10.000 mm at the start and 10.000 mm at the end. Someone concludes that the population is not evolving. Explain what they should be reading instead, and describe what has happened to the allele frequencies underlying beak depth. No calculation.

4. That same run starts with a phenotypic variance of 1.000 mm² and h² = 0.50. The variance falls each generation but never reaches zero, even after thirty generations. State the value it is approaching and explain, in terms of the two components of phenotypic variance, why selection cannot push it below that value in this model.

6On your own

Nothing here is graded. The demonstration does considerably more than this worksheet used.

  • Press Disruptive, strong, then press Run and press Pause at about generation 10. The mean stays at 10.000 mm while the histogram splits into two humps. Watch Relative fitness of an average bird as it happens, and work out why a value below 1.000 is exactly what disruptive selection means.
  • Uncheck Draw the predicted next generation, set up any run you like, and sketch on paper where you think the dashed curve should sit relative to the orange breeders curve. Then switch it back on and see how close you were.
  • Run Disruptive, strong until the distribution is clearly split, then click the Directional tab without pressing Reset. Compare the R tile against the Predicted h² × S tile. R = h²S is exact only while the distribution is bell-shaped, and this one no longer is.

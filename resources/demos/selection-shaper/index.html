Fitness here is relative fitness: each bird's expected contribution of offspring divided by the population average. The dashed horizontal line on the plot is 1.000. Only ratios matter, so the curve is never a survival percentage.

Honest limits

The population is infinite and there is no drift. This is deliberate. The whole distribution is tracked as a smooth density and every number is computed exactly, so anything you see move is selection, not sampling luck. In a real finch population of a few hundred birds the mean would wobble by a few hundredths of a millimetre every generation for no reason at all, and that noise would bury the effect this demonstration exists to show.

What is simplified. Each bird's beak depth is z = g + e: a breeding value g that it passes on, plus a non-heritable deviation e drawn fresh from a normal distribution with variance VE, held fixed for the run. Offspring inherit g unchanged, so h2 = VA/VP is exactly the parent–offspring regression slope. There is no mutation, no recombination or segregation variance and no mating system, which means selection can only reshape the genetic variation already present, never create new variation. That is why stabilizing selection here drives the variance down to the environmental floor VE and stops: with mutation it would settle at a low equilibrium instead of hitting a hard floor. Real disruptive selection also needs mates to pair up assortatively before two peaks can become two species; random mating and blending inheritance would keep filling the valley back in. Finally, R = h2S is exact only while the distribution is bell-shaped — the page warns you when it is not.

What this is for

The misconception: students hear “stabilizing selection” and file it under nothing is happening. The mean does not move, so surely nothing is evolving. Underneath sits a second wrong idea that causes the first: that natural selection is a thing that moves the average, and that the average is the only number a population has. Both are false. A population is a whole distribution, and it has at least two numbers — where it sits and how spread out it is. Selection can hammer the second one while leaving the first untouched.

What to notice. Load the page and press Step generation ten times with Stabilizing selected, strength 0.40 and h2 = 0.50. The mean reads 10.000 mm at generation 0 and 10.000 mm at generation 10 — not approximately, exactly, because the fitness curve is symmetric about the population and the pull from each side cancels. Now read the other tile. The variance has gone from 1.000 mm² to 0.710 mm², a fall of 29%, and the standard deviation from 1.000 mm to 0.843 mm. Every generation, birds with unusually deep or unusually shallow beaks left fewer offspring, and the alleles making beaks unusual got rarer. Allele frequencies changed. That is evolution, by the definition you were given, happening in a population whose average never budged.

Disruptive selection is the mirror image. Press Disruptive, strong and step through. The mean sits at 10.000 mm the whole time — again, symmetry — while the variance climbs past 3 mm² and the histogram splits into two humps with a hollow in the middle. Nothing about the mean tells you that happened; in fact by generation 8 there is barely a bird left at the mean. That is not a made-up scenario: in the African black-bellied seedcracker, birds with small bills eat soft sedge seeds and birds with large bills crack hard ones, and the intermediates are worst at both.

Now separate S from R. S, the selection differential, is measured inside one generation: how much better the birds that actually bred were than the population they came from. R, the response, is measured between generations: how far the mean really moved. They are not the same number, and the gap between them is heritability, R = h2S. Press The h² = 0 test. Selection is switched hard on and it works — the orange curve of breeders is visibly shifted to the right of the blue histogram, and S reads 0.300 mm. But nothing about being a deep-beaked bird was inherited, so R reads 0.000 mm, the dashed next-generation curve lies exactly on top of the current one, and you can press Step forever without the mean moving a micrometre. Selection without heritable variation is not evolution. Turn h2 up to 0.50 and the same selection now gives S = 0.300 mm and R = 0.150 mm: half of what the survivors had is passed on, because half of what made them different was never genetic in the first place.

Where the model stops being true. The population here is infinite, so nothing drifts. There is no mutation, so the variance stabilizing selection removes is gone for good and the run bottoms out at the environmental floor — in a real population, mutation keeps feeding variance back in and the two forces settle at a balance well above zero. And two peaks under disruptive selection are not two species: real birds mate, and blending a large-billed parent with a small-billed one puts offspring right back in the valley. Splitting into species needs the two groups to stop interbreeding, which is a separate story this page does not tell.

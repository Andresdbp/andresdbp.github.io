What this is for

The misconception: students see an H2O drawn next to a new bond and conclude that water is added to glue the monomers together. It is the opposite. One monomer gives up an –OH, the other gives up an –H, those two leftovers walk away as a water molecule, and the atoms that are left behind form the bond. Making a bond gives back a water; breaking that bond uses up a water. Watch the pill in the animation: on “add” it flies away from the chain, on “hydrolyze” it flies in.

What to notice: set the slider to 12. You get 11 bonds and 11 waters, not 12. The first monomer starts the chain for free; every monomer after it brings exactly one bond and releases exactly one water. Then switch tabs. Carbohydrate, protein, and nucleic acid all run the same single reaction — one unit gives up an –OH, the other gives up an –H. What changes is which group hands over the hydrogen (a hydroxyl in sugars and nucleotides, the amino group in a protein) and what the finished bond is called: glycosidic, peptide, phosphodiester. Watch the equation card under the picture change when you pick Protein.

Where the rule breaks — lipids. A fat is not a polymer. A triglyceride is one glycerol plus three fatty acids: 4 molecules, 3 ester bonds, 3 waters. If you treat the fatty acids as “monomers” and use n − 1, you predict 2 waters and you are wrong. The Add button on the lipid tab stops at three on purpose — a triglyceride is full, it cannot grow into a chain the way starch or a protein can.

Honest caveats. Each tab uses one specific monomer so the masses are real numbers you can check: α-D-glucose, glycine, dAMP, palmitic acid. Actual proteins mix 20 different amino acids and actual DNA mixes four nucleotides, so a real chain's mass is a sum of different residues. Also, inside a living cell a DNA or RNA chain is not built by simple dehydration: the polymerase uses nucleoside triphosphates and releases pyrophosphate rather than water. The bond you end up with is the same phosphodiester bond, and the overall atom bookkeeping shown here still balances, but the cell pays for it a different way.

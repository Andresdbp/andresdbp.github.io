5Solve these without the demonstration

Close the page, or look away from it. Show your working on the lines. Then reopen the demonstration, check yourself, and note where any arithmetic went off.

1. A single-stranded DNA fragment is 9 nucleotides long, every one of them dAMP (331.22 Da). How many phosphodiester bonds does it contain, how many water molecules were released in building it, and what is its molar mass to the nearest whole Da? (Water = 18.02 Da.)

2. A polypeptide built entirely from glycine (75.07 Da) has a molar mass of 645.6 Da. How many amino acid residues does it contain, and how many peptide bonds?

3. An amylase completely hydrolyzes one starch fragment of 8 glucose units into 8 free glucose molecules. How many water molecules are consumed? Compare the combined mass of the products with the mass of the original fragment: which is greater, and by how many Da?

4. A triglyceride releases 3 water molecules when it forms, but a chain of 3 glucose monomers releases only 2. Explain the difference in terms of what is being joined to what in each case. Do not simply restate the two rules.

Every mass on this page comes from one specific monomer per family — α-D-glucose, glycine, dAMP, palmitic acid — so that the numbers can be checked by hand. A real protein mixes 20 different amino acids and real DNA mixes four nucleotides, so an actual chain's mass is a sum of different residues, not a multiple of one.

6On your own

Nothing here is graded. The demonstration holds more than this worksheet used.

  • Switch between the four tabs with the equation card in view. Three of them show the same reaction; the Protein tab shows a different one. Work out which group hands over the hydrogen in each case, and why the peptide bond is the exception.
  • On the Lipid tab, take the slider all the way down to 0. Read the caption and the Formula readout for glycerol on its own, then add fatty acids one at a time and track which readouts change and which do not.
  • The note under Build on the Carbohydrate tab says cellulose has the same formula and the same mass per unit as starch. If mass and formula cannot tell them apart, what does — and why can you not digest one of them?
  • Read the last paragraph of “What this is for”, on how a cell actually builds DNA. Decide what the demonstration still gets right about a phosphodiester bond and what it leaves out about the way the cell pays for it.

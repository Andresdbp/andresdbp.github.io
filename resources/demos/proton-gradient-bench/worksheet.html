Open the demonstration. Nothing is added yet: the donor is NADH, substrate supply is 100%, oxygen is about 200 µmol L⁻¹.

  2. Fill in the table from the Live readouts, Where the force actually lives and Energy budget panels.

a. Divide Electron flow by O2 consumption. You should get a whole number. Write the balanced half-reaction at complex IV that explains it.

b. The tiles report magnitudes, so the two components of the force add. Check that Δψ + (59.2 mV × ΔpH) gives the proton-motive force you recorded, and check that Matrix pH − Intermembrane space pH gives your ΔpH. Show both.

c. From the note: protons pumped out ____________ , protons returning through ATP synthase ____________ , protons leaking ____________ (all nmol H⁺ min⁻¹ mg⁻¹). Divide the synthase figure by 4 and compare it with the ATP output you recorded. What does the 4 represent?

The 59.2 mV per pH unit is 2.303 RT/F at 25 °C. It is not a universal constant — at 37 °C the same ΔpH is worth about 61.5 mV. Every number on the bench is quoted at 25 °C.

Nothing in the model treats oligomycin as an inhibitor of the chain. The complexes are untouched; electron flow is not blocked, it is backed up against a gradient the pumps themselves built. Keep that distinction in the wording of your answer.

5Solve these without the demonstration

Close the page or look away. Show working. Then reopen the bench and check yourself — and where you disagree with it, find the step where you diverged.

1. The NADH route pumps 10 H⁺ per electron pair (4 at I, 4 at III, 2 at IV); exporting one ATP costs 4 H⁺ (3 through the rotor, 1 for the ADP/ATP and phosphate carriers). Calculate the theoretical P/O ceiling for NADH and for FADH2. Then, given that the resting bench pumps 1439 nmol H⁺ min⁻¹ mg⁻¹ and only 1324 of them return through the synthase, calculate the P/O you would actually expect to measure.

2. A mitochondrion at 25 °C has a membrane potential of magnitude 172 mV, matrix pH 8.01 and intermembrane space pH 7.20. Calculate ΔpH, its value in millivolts, and the total proton-motive force. Which condition on the bench does this describe?

3. A mitochondrion carries a mutation that makes the inner membrane markedly more permeable to protons. No inhibitor is present and fuel is plentiful. State the direction of change — up, down or unchanged — for pmf, oxygen consumption, ATP output, heat released and P/O ratio, and explain why oxygen consumption moves the way it does even though nothing has been done to any complex. Which of the four treatments does this phenotype imitate?

4. On succinate, an oxygen electrode reports an electron flow of 371 nmol e⁻ min⁻¹ mg⁻¹. Calculate (i) the oxygen consumption rate, (ii) the rate at which protons are pumped out, and (iii) the ATP output you would get if every one of those protons returned through the synthase. The bench actually reports 259 nmol ATP min⁻¹ mg⁻¹. Account for the difference, and check that your two numbers stand in the same ratio as the measured P/O does to its ceiling.

6On your own

Nothing here is graded, and the worksheet has left most of the bench untouched.

  • The Substrate supply slider was never used above. Take it from 100% to 50% to 10% and watch the P/O ratio rather than the rates. Why does starving the chain of fuel change how fast it runs without changing how efficiently it runs?
  • The Oxygen slider is logarithmic, 0.01 to 250 µmol L⁻¹, and complex IV has a Km of 0.5 µmol L⁻¹. Find the concentration at which oxygen consumption has dropped to about half its resting value, and compare it with the Km. How far do you have to drag the slider before anything at all happens, and what does that imply about tissue at 5 to 30 µmol L⁻¹?
  • Add cyanide and watch the recorder trace rather than the tiles. ATP output does not reach zero the instant the chain stops. Read the pmf channel over the same seconds and say what is paying for that last ATP.
  • Read the Honest limits panel. It notes that a real ATP synthase can spin backwards, hydrolysing ATP to defend the gradient. Sketch what the three recorder channels would do after cyanide if this model allowed that, and say which of your answers above it would change.

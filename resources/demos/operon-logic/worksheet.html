1Before you open anything

Answer from what you already know. Whatever you write here gets compared against a number later, so commit to something specific.

Prediction

Wild-type E. coli growing in medium that contains lactose and glucose together. Taking full induction (lactose present, glucose absent) as 100%, roughly what percentage of maximum is lacZYA being transcribed at?

Write one or two sentences saying what reasoning got you that number.

The inducer is allolactose, an isomer that β-galactosidase makes from lactose — not lactose itself. The map draws them as one amber ligand for clarity. Say “allolactose binds the repressor” on paper.

4Using it the way a question would

Exam questions rarely hand you the genotype and ask for the number. They hand you the numbers and ask for the genotype, or they hand you a plausible shortcut and see whether you check it.

A. Predict before you look.

  1. Set Genotype to Oᶜ first, then press Start prediction round. The transcription numbers hide; the molecular map keeps working.
  2. Reading the map alone, choose OFF, LOW or HIGH for each of the four conditions and write your four choices below before you press Reveal.
  3. Press Reveal and record the actual value beside each.

a. Score recorded by the demonstration: ______ / 4. For any box you missed, write what the map was telling you that you did not use.

B. Working backwards from a pattern.

b. A strain reads OFF, OFF, LOW, LOW across the four conditions in that order, and the Repressor on operator readout still responds normally to lactose. Which genotype in the menu is it? Select it and confirm, then write the two features of the pattern that identify it.

C. A shortcut worth testing.

“Once the repressor is gone, nothing is holding the operon back, so it should run at maximum.” That is a reasonable line of thought. Test it.

  1. Set Genotype to lacI⁻ and press lac− glu−. Record the percentage and the Repressor on operator readout.
  2. Press lac− glu+. Record both again.

c. The repressor readout is the same in both rows, yet the transcription level is not. Explain in two sentences why losing the repressor does not make the operon glucose-insensitive.

Negative regulation and positive regulation are separate events on the same promoter. Removing a blocker is not the same thing as supplying a helper, and a strain can lose one while keeping the other intact.

5Solve these without the demonstration

Close the page, or look away from it. Show your reasoning, not just the answer. Then reopen it and check — and where you were wrong, write down which step in the mechanism you skipped.

1. A strain transcribes lacZYA at 100% of maximum in medium with no lactose and no glucose, and at about 4.9% in medium with no lactose but glucose present. Name the two genotypes from the menu that both fit these numbers, and state the experiment that separates them and what the result would be for each.

2. A CAP⁻ strain is grown in lactose with no glucose. Give the transcription level as a percentage of the wild-type maximum, and explain why the number is neither 0% nor 100%.

3. In a lacIˢ strain, glucose is added to a medium that already contains lactose. By what factor does transcription change? Predict the factor and justify it before you check — and say why it is the same factor as in wild type even though the absolute levels differ by more than a hundredfold.

4. Explanation only, no numbers. Explain why deleting the repressor leaves the operon fully glucose-sensitive, while deleting CAP makes the glucose signal disappear entirely. Your answer should name what each protein does to the promoter and say which one glucose acts through.

6On your own

Nothing here is graded. The second tab, Eukaryotic enhancers, is untouched by everything above, and it is where this unit is going.

  • Switch to Eukaryotic enhancers and cycle the Which cell are you in? menu through hepatocyte, skeletal muscle cell and neuron. Record all three transcription levels, then use the Silencer occupied readout to say why muscle and neuron are both “off” for different reasons — and why the neuron sits lower.
  • Select the neuron, then tick HNF-4α and C/EBPα. The gene barely moves. Now untick MeCP2. What does the order of those two results tell you about a repressor sitting on top of a set of activators? Press Back to the real cell when you are done.
  • Starting from Sp1 alone, add HNF-4α on its own and note the level; go back and add C/EBPα on its own and note that level; then have both bound at once. Add the two separate increases together and compare with the level for both. Then select the skeletal muscle cell and read the tag beside the MyoD checkbox — it states why an abundant and genuinely powerful activator does nothing to this particular gene.

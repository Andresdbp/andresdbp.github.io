1Before you open anything

Answer from what you already know about epinephrine binding a G protein-coupled receptor. Commit to a number before the demonstration shows you one.

Prediction

Ten molecules of hormone bind ten receptors on one liver cell. The signal passes through four steps: receptor → G protein → cAMP → activated enzyme → product. Roughly how many product molecules does that cell end up making? Give an order of magnitude — tens, thousands, millions.

Write one sentence saying how you got that number — what you assumed each step does to the count.

5Solve these without the demonstration

Close the page, or look away from it. Show your working, in scientific notation where it helps. Then reopen the demonstration, set it up, and check yourself — and if you were wrong, write down where it went off.

1. A four-tier cascade starts with 40 hormone molecules bound. The tier factors are ×50, ×200, ×5 and ×2,000, and the terminators are at normal speed. Find the total gain and the number of product molecules.

2. Take the standard cascade — 10 hormone molecules bound and factors ×20, ×100, ×10, ×1,000 — which delivers 2.0×108 product molecules at normal terminator speed. The terminators are now running five times faster. Find the new product count without recomputing tier by tier.

3. A cell needs 6.0×108 product molecules and has only 30 hormone molecules bound. Its first three tiers give ×20, ×100 and ×10, and the terminators are at normal speed. What total gain is required, and what must the fourth tier factor be?

4. Someone proposes inserting a fifth protein into the pathway, one that is activated by the tier above it and activates exactly one molecule of the tier below. Explain what that insertion does to the number of product molecules, what it does to the time the response takes, and how the top plot would show it. No calculation is needed, but your answer must use the word gain.

6On your own

Nothing here is graded. The demonstration does more than this worksheet uses.

  • Drag Hormone molecules bound from 1 to 100 while watching both Total gain and Product molecules from the cascade. One of them moves and one does not. Explain which is which, then confirm it against the equation you wrote in part 3b.
  • Using the lower plot, work out how fast the terminators would have to run to bring the cascade output down to the ten molecules a bucket brigade delivers. Check whether that setting exists on the slider, and say what that tells you about how hard it is to silence a cascade purely by speeding up termination.
  • The hint under tier 3 says it takes four cAMP to release the catalytic subunits of one protein kinase A, yet the tier factor is a net ×10. Set tier 3 to ×1 and notice the slider stops there. Decide what a factor below one would have to mean physically, and whether a real step could have one.
  • Turn off Show the bucket-brigade comparison, then set all four tiers to ×1 by hand rather than with the button, and read the caption that appears under the written-out multiplication.

What this is for

The tempting move: treat a conversion as one decision — multiply or divide — pick the one that feels right, get a number, and check the back of the book to find out whether the pick was correct. That works often enough to feel reliable, which is exactly why it survives. It also leaves the answer key as the only thing judging the work.

What to notice: flip any car the wrong way and watch what happens. The units stop cancelling, and you end up with something like km²/m instead of m. A calculator will still hand you a number — it just isn't an answer to the question you were asked. Nothing about the arithmetic tells you that; only the units do. In problem 4 you cannot cancel cm³ with one cm: you need the centimeter-to-meter factor three times, or the cm³-to-m³ factor once. That is not a rule to memorize — it is what "per cubic centimeter" means.

The honest caveat: cancelling units proves your setup is right. It cannot prove your facts are right. Every factor in the bank is a true, exact definition, so here correct units always give the correct number. Type a false factor into the custom box — say 1 km = 100 m — and the units will still cancel beautifully while the answer is wrong. The demo will tell you when that happens.

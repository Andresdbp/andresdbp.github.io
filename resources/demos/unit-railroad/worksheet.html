1Before you open anything

Answer from what you already know, with the page closed. The point is to have a commitment on paper that the demonstration can later confirm or overturn.

Prediction

A car is traveling 55.0 mi/hr. Using 1 mi = 1.609344 km, write the same speed in m/s to three significant figures.

Now the part that matters: you had to deal with hours and seconds. Say in one sentence how you decided whether to multiply or divide by 3,600, and what told you that was the right choice.

3Which way up does a factor go?

Choose 3. mi/hr → m/s. Build it one car at a time, and after every single action read the two boxes before doing anything else. Add the factors in the order listed; each one goes in unflipped first, then gets flipped.

  1. With an empty chain, record row 1.
  2. Add 1 mi / 1.609344 km. Record row 2, then flip that car and record row 3. Leave it in whichever orientation cancels mi.
  3. Add 1 km / 1,000 m. Record row 4, then flip it and record row 5. Leave it in whichever orientation cancels km.
  4. Add 1 hr / 3,600 s. Record row 6, then flip it and record row 7.

a. Two of the three factors had to be flipped and one did not. Which one went in correctly as the bank supplied it, and what was different about where its unit started?

b. State the rule in your own words: given a unit you want to get rid of, how do you know which side of the new factor it belongs on? Do not use the words “flip” or “top and bottom” without saying relative to what.

c. Compare the finished answer with your prediction in Part 1. If the number matched, say whether your reasoning about the 3,600 matches the rule you just wrote. If it did not match, identify the single step where the two disagree.

Every factor in the bank is an exact definition, not a measurement, so none of them limits precision. The three significant figures in the answer come from the 55.0 mi/hr you were given, and from nothing else.

4Two ways the setup can look right and not be

Both of these are shortcuts that work in other problems. Here is where each one stops working.

A. A cubed unit is not one unit. Choose 4. g/cm³ → kg/m³.

  1. Add 1 m / 100 cm and flip it so that cm sits on top. Record row 1.
  2. Add the same factor a second time, flipped the same way. Record row 2.
  3. Add it a third time, flipped the same way. Record row 3.
  4. Now finish the conversion by adding 1 kg / 1,000 g in whichever orientation cancels g. Record row 4.

a. After row 1 the surviving units contained cm to a power. Which power, and why did one cm on top not remove cm³ from the bottom?

b. Press Clear chain and rebuild the same conversion in two cars, using 1 m³ / 1,000,000 cm³ instead of three copies of the length factor. Does the Number box end up the same? Show where the 1,000,000 comes from, starting from 1 m = 100 cm.

B. Cancelling units checks the setup, not the facts. Go back to 1. km → m and press Clear chain.

  1. Open Type your own factor. Set the top to 1 and km, the bottom to 100 and m. Press Add to bank.
  2. Click your new factor in the bank and flip it so the km cancels.

c. Record the Units surviving, the Number, and the tag on the dashed car.

d. The units cancelled perfectly and the answer is still wrong. State precisely what a complete cancellation does prove, and what it cannot prove.

The demonstration compares the number against an independently computed answer, which is why it can catch a false equality. On paper nothing does that for you — cancelled units mean your arrangement is right, and the equalities themselves are still yours to get right. Your typed factor stays in the bank for every problem until you reload the page.

5Solve these without the demonstration

Close the page. Write every conversion factor as a fraction and show the cancellations before you touch a calculator. Then reopen the demonstration and check — if a number disagrees, find the car where the two setups differ.

1. Convert 2.50 km to inches, using 1 in = 2.54 cm exactly. Give the answer to three significant figures. (Check against problem 2.)

2. A car gets 32.0 mi/gal. Using 1 mi = 1.609344 km and 1 gal = 3.785411784 L, convert this to km/L to three significant figures. Say which of the two units you dealt with first and why the order did not change the answer. (Check against problem 5.)

3. Without computing anything, decide what units survive this chain:

2.50 km × (1 km / 1,000 m) × (1 m / 100 cm) × (2.54 cm / 1 in)

Then state the smallest change that would make it produce inches.

4. Explain, without doing any arithmetic, why both 1 mi / 1.609344 km and 1.609344 km / 1 mi may be used in a conversion chain, and what that fact means about the effect each one has on the quantity as opposed to the units.

6On your own

Nothing here is graded, and the demonstration holds more than this worksheet used.

  • Redo problem 3 without the mi–km factor, going mi → ft → in → cm → m instead. Count the cars, then compare the final number with the short route. Should the two agree exactly, or only to three significant figures?
  • Get a chain deliberately stuck, then press What has to cancel next? It names one unit and one side. Work out how it could know that without knowing the answer.
  • Problem 4 offers 1 mL / 1 cm³ and 1 L / 1,000 mL, which the shortest route never needs. Build a chain that passes through litres on the way and still lands on kg/m³. Then say what makes the mL–cm³ factor unlike every other factor in the bank, and what that implies about the detour you just took.
  • Type a factor with a unit that appears nowhere in the problem and add it to a finished chain. Predict what the two boxes will say before you look.

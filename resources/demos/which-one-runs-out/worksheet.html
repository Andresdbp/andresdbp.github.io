1Before you open anything

Answer from what you already know, with the page closed. The point is to have something on paper that the demonstration can later settle.

Prediction

You mix 20.0 g of N2 with 5.0 g of H2 and let them react as far as they can: N2 + 3 H2 → 2 NH3. Which reactant is completely used up, and which one is still in the flask at the end?

Write one sentence saying what you compared to decide that.

5Solve these without the demonstration

Close the page or look away. Show your working. Then reopen it, set the sliders to the masses given, and check yourself — if a number is off, write down where. Molar masses: N2 28.014, H2 2.016, NH3 17.031 g/mol.

1. 28.0 g of N2 is mixed with 4.00 g of H2. Identify the limiting reactant, the theoretical yield of NH3 in grams, and the mass of the excess reactant left over.

2. For a mixture of 60.0 g N2 and 12.0 g H2, someone argues: “There are 2.14 mol of N2 and 5.95 mol of H2, so nitrogen is limiting.” Both mole values are correct. Explain what is wrong with the conclusion, and give the reactant that is actually limiting.

3. You have 35.0 g of N2 and want to convert all of it, using as little excess hydrogen as possible. What mass of H2 do you need, and what mass of NH3 would you get? When you check, round the hydrogen up to the nearest 0.1 g so the slider can reach it.

4. A mixture contains equal masses of N2 and H2. Without a calculator, argue which reactant must be limiting, whatever that common mass is. Your argument should use both the molar masses and the coefficients.

Every yield on this sheet is a theoretical yield: the ceiling if the reaction ran to completion with no side reactions. Ammonia synthesis is an equilibrium, and one pass through an industrial reactor at about 450 °C and 200 atm converts only roughly 15% of the feed, with the rest recycled. Percent yield compares a measured mass to the ceiling you calculated.

6On your own

Nothing here is graded. The demonstration does more than this worksheet used.

  • Tick Predict first — hide the answers, load a preset you have not used, and work the limiting reactant out from the comparison table alone before you untick it.
  • Watch the particle picture as you move the sliders. Its caption tells you how many moles one drawn molecule stands for. Find a mixture where the leftover reactant vanishes from the picture even though the mass panel still reports a leftover, and explain what the drawing is rounding.
  • The preset All three agree puts 80.0 g of N2 against 4.0 g of H2. Starting there, find the smallest change to one slider that makes a green chip turn red.
  • Take a theoretical yield from anywhere on this sheet and work out what mass of NH3 a single industrial pass at roughly 15% conversion would actually deliver.

1Before you open anything

Answer from what you already know, before the screen tells you anything. The point is to have a number on paper to argue with later.

Prediction

Propane burns according to C3H8 + 5 O2 → 3 CO2 + 4 H2O. You burn 25.0 g of propane and every gram of it reacts. Is the mass of CO2 produced more than, less than, or equal to 25.0 g? Commit to an actual number of grams.

Write one or two sentences on how you got that number — including anything you assumed without checking.

  2. Record the Molar mass and Atoms per unit readouts, then read the Share of mass column of the Atom by atom table and record whichever element holds the largest share.
  3. Repeat for the other three formulas in the table. Those three are preset buttons under the formula box; C3H8 is the only one you have to type.

Under the Amount box the demonstration reports how many significant figures your entry carries and rounds the answer to match. Typing 25 instead of 25.0 changes the reported answer even though the arithmetic is identical. The atomic masses it uses are the IUPAC 2021 conventional values, and a molar mass built by addition keeps the fewest decimal places of any atomic mass in it — that is why Al2(SO4)3 stops at two decimals while Ca(OH)2 keeps three.

The substance you started with is a product this time, so the middle arrow runs the other way. Write the mole ratio you used, with the correct quantity on top, and say which coefficient supplied each number.

Read the panel headed What this assumes beside the readouts. Every number this level produces assumes the reaction runs to completion at 100 percent yield, with the starting substance completely consumed. Nothing on this worksheet is a prediction of what a real flask gives you — limiting reactant and percent yield are what close that gap.

5Solve these without the demonstration

Close the page or look away from it. Show every conversion factor as a fraction, with units. Then reopen the demonstration and check yourself — and if a number is off, find the step it went wrong at rather than just correcting the total.

1. Find the molar mass of Ca(OH)2, then the percentage of its mass that is oxygen. Give the molar mass to the correct number of decimal places for a sum, and the percentage to two decimal places.

2. The thermite reaction is 2 Al + Fe2O3 → Al2O3 + 2 Fe. What mass of iron is produced from 50.0 g of Fe2O3, assuming complete reaction? Report to three significant figures.

3. How many oxygen atoms are in 25.0 g of Al2(SO4)3? Note that the demonstration will report formula units, so one step of this is yours alone — state which.

4. A shortcut worth taking seriously: divide the grams of A by the molar mass of A and multiply by the molar mass of B, with nothing in between. For CH4 + 2 O2 → CO2 + 2 H2O it gives the right mass of CO2 every time. For 2 KClO3 → 2 KCl + 3 O2 it does not give the right mass of O2. Explain why it survives the first reaction, and state the exact factor by which it is wrong in the second.

6On your own

Nothing here is graded, and none of it appeared above. The demonstration does considerably more than this worksheet uses.

  • On level 1, type CuSO4*5H2O and then CuSO4. What fraction of a hydrate's mass is water, and what happens to a mass measurement if the crystals are damp?
  • Still on level 1, compare CO with Co. Decide what each one is before you look at the molar mass.
  • On level 2, cycle the Substance through Fe, NaCl and CH4 with Find set to particles. The noun on the answer changes each time. Work out why NaCl is not counted in molecules.
  • On level 4, try Photosynthesis (overall) and then Decomposition of potassium chlorate with every species shown. Find a reaction where the total moles of product exceed the total moles of reactant, and one where they fall.
